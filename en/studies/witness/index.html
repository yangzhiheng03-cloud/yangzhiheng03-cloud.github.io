The Witness: Breaking Down the Opening Gate Puzzle

  • The Witness
  • Environmental teaching
  • Visual guidance
  • Puzzle feedback

Three cables and a locked gate: how the opening area teaches players to identify a goal, test a rule, and search for answers.

1. How the game works

The panels in The Witness share a goal. Start at the circle, draw a line that follows the rules of the puzzle, and reach an endpoint.

2. The level I chose

I chose the gate in the opening area that takes three powered cables to unlock. It is a deduction puzzle. The player has drawn lines on panels before. Now they have to work out what a line on a panel has to do with a gate in the landscape.

3. How the puzzle fits together

(1) The goal is to pass through the gate.

(2) The lock is the panel beside the gate. Three shutters cover it.

(3) The keys are the three panels at the other ends of the cables.

4. The mechanics

(1) Draw a line on a panel. The player plans a route from the circle to the endpoint.

(2) Complete the line and the cable attached to the panel lights up.

(3) The other end of each cable leads to the panel beside the gate. Power a cable and the corresponding shutter opens.

5. Visual guidance through the puzzle

The opening

(1) What draws the player in. On entering, the player sees a panel like the one in the preceding tutorial. They know what to do with it and can walk straight up and try it.

(2) Reading the goals

① Main goal. Several cables converge on the panel beside the gate. Even before the player knows where each cable leads, they can see which spot they will eventually return to.

② Secondary goals. The other panels are scattered around the area. Cables give the player a direction to search in, while walls and vegetation hide the panels themselves. From the gate, the player can see what they want to open, but only part of what it will take.

From the opening into the middle

(3) Establishing the rule. The cable outside the panel lights up only when the line connects the start to an endpoint. The result of an action on the panel appears out in the landscape, where the player can follow it.

(4) How the connections between parts of the puzzle emerge

① From the entrance, the first thing the player sees is a familiar panel. As soon as they finish drawing, the cable beside them lights up. One action teaches them how to solve this panel and what solving it changes in the world.

② Following the lit cable takes the player past the areas containing the second and third puzzles. They start to pay attention to the other cables and the paths branching off along the way.

③ Back at the gate, the top shutter is open. Two are still closed below it. The player can work backwards from what they just did and set their own next goal. Find two more panels and power the two remaining cables.

The middle

(5) Feedback

① Completing a panel lights the nearby cable. The player can see immediately that the line worked.

② The matching shutter beside the gate opens. Walking back shows the player exactly which part they unlocked.

③ The shutter makes a sound as it opens. Even with their back to the gate, the player can hear something move in the distance and has a reason to turn around.

(6) Changing the viewpoint

① Looking for the last panel, the player follows the cable as before and runs into a wall. The cable continues. The player has to stop, turn, and find another way around.

② The first puzzle already offered a glimpse of this explorable space. When the wall cuts off the obvious route, somewhere the player saw earlier but never visited becomes a lead.

③ The last panel has two solutions. The easier line to draw unlocks a mechanism in a later area. The cable to the gate stays dark, but something overhead clicks open. The player knows the gate is still locked and that their action worked somewhere else. Drawing a different route opens the gate in front of them.

6. Three things that affect difficulty

(1) How well the key objects are hidden. A wall cuts across the last stretch of cable. The question shifts from “Can I follow this line?” to “I know where it goes. How do I get there?”

(2) The structure of the puzzle. The start and end are easy to recognize. What takes thought is the path between them and which exit that path will power.

(3) The variety of outcomes. The last panel connects one solution to the gate and the other to a later mechanism. The same drawing rule ties puzzles in two areas together.

7. What I took from this level

(1) The role of the level

① This is a first group of puzzles for a new player.

② What I want from this group is a short, complete play loop. The player observes the space, infers a connection, tries it on a panel, and reads the environment to decide what to do next.

(2) How the level progresses

① Early on, a familiar panel invites the player to draw the first line and discover that the cable lights up.

② In the middle, the first cable leads to the gate. The two remaining shutters let the player set the goal of finding two more panels.

③ Towards the end, the wall interrupts the cable chase and calls for exploration. The final panel then offers a clue to another mechanism.

④ First I want to know, “How do I open this gate?” Later I am left wondering, “What did I just unlock?” Each question arrives when I need a reason to keep playing.

(3) What I would use in another introductory level

① A small goal can take a new player through the whole game loop once. Between seeing this gate and opening it, the player does something they will return to throughout the game.

② Unknowns can arrive in stages. Put a panel within reach so the player can try something immediately. Once the rule is familiar, a wall or the distant sound of a mechanism can introduce the next question.

③ Use familiar experiences to teach a new rule. A cable lighting up is worth following. One shutter gone leaves two to deal with. Players can spot these relationships for themselves and test them on the next panel.

(4) I think this puzzle works as an introduction because the single goal of opening a gate can guide the player, teach the rules, and give them a reason to keep exploring.
