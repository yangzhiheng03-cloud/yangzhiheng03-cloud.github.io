MECHANICS & ONBOARDING

Animal Well: one tool, many possibilities

  • Tool mechanics
  • Environmental interaction
  • Level onboarding

How temporary footholds, repeated use, and environmental interactions let a single tool support exploration.

This short study is adapted from my Animal Well research for Anchor. It focuses on the bubble tool and its learning sequence, then traces their influence on my design.

Start with an intuitive use

A bubble can support the player, turning an empty position in the air into a temporary platform. Standing on it establishes an immediately understandable relationship: a bubble can be a foothold.

For Anchor, this suggested giving the tool a directly observable spatial use beyond throwing and impact: the player can stand on an anchor that has landed or embedded in a wall.

Let repetition create an advanced technique

Jumping from one bubble and creating another to catch the player enables repeated ascent. The advanced technique combines existing actions and asks players to rethink when to use the same tool.

Fish-head pipes hold bubbles, and touching the held bubble lets the player enter the pipe. The tool has a relationship with the environment as well as with the character. This encouraged me to explore the anchor through weight, attachment, throwing, and connection.

Let players experience it before creating it

Before obtaining the bubble wand, the player encounters bubbles produced by a seahorse. After obtaining the tool, they can create their own footholds. Experiencing an interaction in the environment before gaining the ability to create it became a useful learning sequence for my project.

In Anchor, I structured learning around spatial problems: encounter a height difference, a water surface, or a wall, then discover a use for the tool that changes what is possible.

Bring the lesson into a different level

The transferable idea is the relationship between a tool and its space. Bubbles provide temporary platforms; the anchor adds weight, a chain, and water. On revisiting the pool, the same space means something different because the player understands a new combination.

Explore a mechanic in depth: establish an intuitive use, then let the space pose a new problem.
