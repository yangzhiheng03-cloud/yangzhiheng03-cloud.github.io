Wuthering Waves 3.0: A Level Study of Bjartr Woods, Lahai-Roi

  • Wuthering Waves
  • Open world
  • Route design
  • Exploration pacing

Tracing routes around Haldarheim, the Soliseed Plains, and the Solisylum to examine how landmarks, sightlines, traversal, and branches organize free exploration.

I. The gameplay loop II. How the level unfolds III. How the level guides exploration IV. What I took from the level

A Wuthering Waves map has to let players choose their own direction and give them something to do whichever way they go. I came to Bjartr Woods with two questions. When a player finishes the challenge in front of them, how do they discover the next one? And what does the same map feel like to someone heading straight for the destination and someone who wants to collect everything along the way?

I. The gameplay loop

Over the long term, players advance Rover’s story and develop a team. In the medium term, they raise their exploration progress and prepare for difficult encounters. On a short outing, that comes down to a material to gather, a fight, a device to operate, or a stretch of ground worth riding across. I wanted to see how Bjartr Woods links those short-term goals.

II. How the level unfolds

I divide the woods into Haldarheim, the Soliseed Plains, and the Solisylum. The floating Solistrees around them mainly serve a side quest, with a more contained, linear sequence. My route stays on the ground, moving from Haldarheim across the plains to the temple.

The three areas have different jobs. Haldarheim introduces the region, the plains open it up for exploration, and the Solisylum gives the player a destination they can recognize from far away. I look at what each offers, how the difficulty changes, and where players are likely to slow down.

1. Haldarheim

The huge stone ring is the first thing that stands out. The tents, the field of flowers, and the ring define a transition area with clear edges. Players have time to get their bearings before entering the main part of the woods.

Most of what there is to find here consists of treasure spots, materials, and a small beacon. In the setting, the animals here have been domesticated. They do not attack as the player passes, leaving attention free for the light gate and the motorcycle.

The busiest moment is riding through the light gate and collecting the supply chests along the route. Then the bike lands in a field of flowers. A moment ago I was chasing rewards. Now I can stop and look. This is the first high point in Haldarheim. The rest of the approach gives me time to get to know the camp and the ring.

2. The Soliseed Plains

Giant sunflowers and bright yellow-green light mark the main part of the plains. It looks untouched by the Void Storm. Away from the main route the palette shifts to purple. The player gets a hint of the change in territory before encountering a new challenge.

From the Resonance Nexus onward, the plains and the temple form one continuous outing. Both main routes place a golden Tidal Heritage towards the end. Fights punctuate each route, with collectibles, traversal, and exploration challenges in between. The difficulty rises over the full route, with dips along the way.

If I treat the end of a challenge as the start of the next leg, I can usually see a collectible, a path, or a high point worth investigating from where I finish. That handoff leaves players free to pick a direction while giving them somewhere to look when a short stretch is over.

Both routes offer ways into secondary areas. Players can stay on the main path, or turn off to find out what is in the darker part of the map.

3. The Solisylum

Above the temple hangs a sphere that absorbs light. The building opens like a book to reveal the central observatory. I can recognize that silhouette from a distance. It gives the landmark I have seen repeatedly on the way here a precise destination.

I placed the beats from the route analysis below on the horizontal axis, assigned a weight to the challenges at each one, and added them up to trace the rises and falls. The three figures show the scoring key, the events at each beat, and the curve for the route to the Soliseed return monument.

In my playthrough, the route keeps building after several dips. The first path feels more like a linear side quest and eventually returns to the monument. The third has a short main path and puts more of its exploration off to the side. I follow the second path below to look at the transitions between beats.

One thing my chart undercounts is the distance between beats. After the first view of the Solisylum, for instance, there is a long motorcycle ride to the Nexus. It has few challenges, yet it gives players just enough time to get used to the new region and prepare to explore. That ride should occupy space on a pacing chart too.

III. How the level guides exploration

1. Guidance along the route

Composition and guiding objects. As I approach each beat, I look first at the object most likely to catch my eye. Then I look for where the next goal appears once I finish what is in front of me. The woods use this relationship repeatedly. I approach a landmark, and something closer draws me onto the next stretch of path.

Finding the way through Haldarheim

1.1 On the way to the woods, a huge stone ring stands out from its surroundings. It looks like an entrance, but all I can see inside is a faint transparent effect. I know I can go through before I know what will happen on the other side.

1.2 Standing in front of the entrance to Haldarheim, I read the view in three layers.

Foreground: The ascending steps give me a direction. An opening at the top leaves empty space that makes me wonder what is behind it.

Middle distance: Purple plants and rocks set the mood. A motorcycle interaction hints at how I will move through the next section.

Background: The giant ring frames the space beyond and tells me the staircase is just the entrance.

1.3–1.8 · From the camp through the flower field to the ring

1.3 Past the stone entrance, an NPC and a patterned pillar draw me to the end of the road. After the conversation, the camera pans to either side. The tents are close, and the gate that summons the bike is farther away. Distance offers an easy order of exploration. Look at the tents, then head for the gate.

1.4 Riding through the gate, I can pick up all the chests in one uninterrupted movement. The run of rewards gives the route its first lift.

1.5 I land facing a whole field of flowers. A moment ago I needed to watch the route and the chests. Now I can look into the distance and move into the field at my own pace. This is the first scenic high point after entering Haldarheim.

1.6 Turning the camera in the flower field reveals two ways forward. One heads behind the ring, with the destination still hidden. The other runs towards the centre of Haldarheim, with NPCs along it and a building at the end. The Nexus is still inactive. A completionist can investigate the visible camp first, while a player curious about the ring can go straight through. Both routes lead to the same area.

1.7 Beyond the flowers, only a few materials sit within easy reach of the path. Players can pick them up without stopping for another busy challenge. The pace settles, leaving room for the next reveal.

1.8 The stone ring fills most of the frame. The passage at its centre opens onto a blank space. That empty space conceals the destination for a moment and makes me want to see what will appear when I cross it.

1.9 Only after entering the passage do the buildings gradually come into view. A settlement with a technological feel fills the gap, and a viewpoint ahead brings the reveal to its peak. The bike also slows through here. I read that slowdown as time for the scene to load and time for the player to take it in.

Foreground: A wide stretch of grass establishes where I am standing.

Middle distance: Giant sunflowers fill the view and give the plains a sense of life.

Background: The silhouette of the Solisylum marks the destination for the whole outing.

Finding the way across the plains to the Solisylum

1.10–1.11 · From the first temple view to the Nexus

1.10 When I bring my gaze back from the temple, the road ahead is straight, with no obstacles that require a stop. Grapple points reinforce the direction of travel. I naturally call up the bike. As the ambient sound changes, the excitement of the panorama subsides and I start watching for things to explore.

1.11 After riding for a while, the immediate surroundings have become familiar, but I still have no clear picture of the area as a whole. The Nexus appears at the end of the road. Activating it reveals the regional map. My question changes from “Where am I?” to “Where do I want to go next?”

1.12 The Nexus offers three directions. All three can eventually lead back to the temple I saw earlier.

First, start the side quest Where Light Takes Shape and follow it up to the floating structures overhead.

Second, head for the Soliseed return monument. The side quest eventually brings the player back here, and a clear map icon marks the place to exchange collectibles for rewards.

Third, stay on the main road and go directly to the Solisylum. This is the fastest route, but mist in the distance makes the destination less immediately attractive and gives nearby points of interest a chance to catch the eye.

The first path eventually reaches the second path’s destination when the quest ends. I continue from the monument below.

1.13–1.17 · From the hilltop collectible into darker ground

1.13 On leaving the shepherd, a collectible above me draws my eye, and a nearby grapple point shows how to get there. A pool narrows the space I could ride straight across, turning me towards the collectible and the climb.

1.14 At the high point, the Solisylum comes back into the centre of the view. I can read a near goal and a distant one at the same time.

Foreground: A grapple leads towards the next Sonance Casket.

Background: The temple shows where the route ultimately goes.

1.15 At the next casket, a purple Tidal Heritage catches my attention. When the fight is over, a rhinoceros and a deer are fighting ahead of me. Their movement takes over the attention the battle has just freed up and draws me in the direction marked by the arrow.

1.16 Once past the rhino, a bike ramp and an open stretch of grass come into view. I can relax the focus the fight needed, get back on the bike, and gather speed towards the next point of interest.

1.17 The ramp launches me into an unfamiliar, much darker area. The approach was bright and open. By the time the wheels touch down, the light has changed. The movement itself carries me through the scene transition.

1.18 Riding on, I pass an altar. Looking up reveals a Sonance Casket above a hollow, but at speed I can easily pass underneath it. “There’s something up there that I haven’t reached” gives me a spot to remember on the return trip.

1.19 Farther along, a chest requires a fight and a projection puzzle, giving me a reason to get off the bike. After the fight, I climb towards a casket. Only from that height can I see the grapple point leading up over the hollow. The collectible I passed a moment ago now has a route I can actually follow.

1.20–1.21 · From the fork to the beacon at the temple

1.20 The way ahead forks. On the left, a golden Tidal Heritage marks the final challenge and reward on this stretch. The temple sits directly in the background. Finishing this part of the route feels like getting close to the destination.

1.21 After the challenge, an easier route along the water curves around to the front of the temple and a small beacon. The route comes together at the temple doors.

1.22 The third path from the Nexus goes straight to a different golden Tidal Heritage. Its final challenge is comparable to the one on the second path, and the way on towards the temple is easy to read.

1.23 Branches of different sizes run alongside both main routes. Players can step off to pursue something of their own choosing and find their way back once they have it.

Take the space above the hollow in 1.19. Reaching it gives me a look at the back of the Solisylum and a glimpse of a later area. There are few more challenges up here. Once I take the collectible, I have a reason to rejoin the main route.

Now look at the large explorable area beside the third path. The direct road to the temple is short, but there is ample space to explore alongside it. Players who take this route still get to choose how long to spend here.

2. Guiding through opportunities

The woods offer enough routes and solutions to let me follow my own interests. Whichever way I go, there is something to pick up the thread and a reward worth reaching.

2.1 Challenges and rewards

Both paths end in a golden Tidal Heritage. My choice at the Nexus determines what I see along the way and how I get there. Each route offers a comparable payoff.

2.2 Side paths

The second route passes through more beats of its own and has smaller branches. The third reaches the destination quickly but has a larger optional area alongside it. Looking at the main paths and their branches together shows how the amount of exploration balances out.

IV. What I took from the level

One main passage connects Bjartr Woods to the surrounding region. Once through the ring, I can read the woods as a relatively self-contained space. When a landmark appears, where riding speeds up, and where I come to a stop can all be arranged around a rhythm particular to this area.

It reminded me of Stormveil Castle in Elden Ring. Stormveil in Limgrave and Bjartr Woods in Lahai-Roi share a layout idea. Once inside, the main area opens into two principal routes, with branches on either side. The distinction I find interesting is the price the player pays for taking the shorter road.

The front approach to Stormveil is shorter, but it demands considerable skill. A newcomer will struggle to get straight through. In Bjartr Woods the short route gets me to the end sooner. Its last challenge is close in difficulty to the one on the long route, and I can ride past it for now. Taking a shortcut here mainly changes how long I explore and what I see.

I think this comes from the expectations each game builds. Over a long period of exploring Wuthering Waves, players get used to treating the challenges and rewards they see as things they can eventually complete. An area can be geographically self-contained and still need to meet the difficulty expectations set elsewhere. Bjartr Woods can borrow variations in route and pace from single-player open worlds while keeping the final challenges on both paths within a similar range.
