Overview
A sailor explores an interconnected platforming level with an anchor and chain. The same anchor can make a foothold, hold a connection or open a route through water.
Goals

INSPIRATION & RESEARCH

INSPIRATION

2026 CiGA · 48-hour Game Jam · Two weeks of iteration

Three teammates and I made this game around the theme “Anchor” at the 2026 CiGA Game Jam. I designed the mechanics and levels and built the playable spaces. We developed the game in 48 hours, then spent two weeks refining the mechanics and levels. The bubbles in Animal Well inspired me. You can stand on one to jump, or make a series of bubbles to reach higher ground and open new routes. I wanted a single anchor to have that same range. By experimenting, players can turn it into a foothold, break walls and, once they attach a chain, dive underwater or launch themselves into the air.

MIND MAP

I started with four properties of an anchor: its weight, its ability to hold things in place, the way it can be thrown and the things it can connect. I built obstacles and routes around those properties so players could discover more uses for the same tool by experimenting, combining actions and revisiting rooms.

Exploration: observe → experiment → combine → revisit. Collectibles reward a closer look at the environment and practice with advanced techniques.

CASE STUDY / ANIMAL WELL

01 / Art direction

Bright details stand out against dark backgrounds.

I studied the pixel art and silhouettes, using different colours for rock, water and characters to distinguish the things players can interact with.

02.1 / A temporary foothold

A bubble can support the player, making a temporary platform in midair.

Standing on a bubble is easy to understand. Players can see what it does and try it straight away.

02.2 / Chaining jumps

Jump off one bubble and make another to catch yourself. Repeating the action lets you keep climbing.

A familiar move gives players an idea for a more advanced technique that can solve a new problem.

02.3 / Environmental interactions

A fish-head pipe draws in a bubble. Touch the bubble and you can enter the pipe.

Giving the tool a way to interact with its surroundings adds another use beyond its basic function.

03 / Level design

Before finding the Bubble Wand, players encounter bubbles made by a seahorse. Once they have the wand, they can make their own footholds.

I borrowed that teaching sequence. Players experience an interaction in the environment first, then get the tool that lets them create the same effect themselves.

PERSONA

DESIGN INTENTION

I wanted to build about ten minutes of exploration around the anchor in a compact, interconnected space. Players begin by making footholds and breaking walls, learn to use tension and sink underwater, then return to familiar rooms to find new routes. Reaching the exit completes the game. Collectibles give players another reason to observe closely and experiment.

GAME MECHANICS & LEVEL STRUCTURE

Basic mechanics

ANCHOR

Left-click to throw · Aim in any direction with the mouse

You can stand on the anchor after it lands or lodges in a wall. Throwing it breaks fragile walls. Thick walls require a drop from sufficient height. Only one anchor can exist at a time. Throwing it again replaces the previous anchor and chain.

CHAIN

Once you collect the chain, throwing the anchor connects it to you.

When the chain goes taut, you travel with the anchor and can launch yourself upward. Once the anchor stops, use the mouse wheel to shorten or extend the chain. Hook the anchor and reel yourself onto a platform. The length is fixed while you are moving.

Right-click to detach

The anchor and chain stay in the room until the next throw replaces them.

WATER

Water pushes you up. The anchor sinks.

You can swim sideways, but buoyancy keeps pushing you back to the surface. Attach yourself to the sinking anchor to go underwater and move along the bottom.

A new use on the return trip

The pool you could only swim across on your first visit becomes an entrance once you understand how the anchor and chain work together.

LEVEL DESIGN METHOD

A ten-minute exploration level

Changes in height, pools and walls give players different ways to use the same ability. Understanding how it works opens the route. The level uses four elements: an anchor, a chain, water and breakable walls.

Reflection

Working with the team showed me how much a mechanic can change once someone actually plays it. A mechanic on paper still needs to be tested in a playable space. Seeing what happens and getting feedback lets me refine the experience.

When the programmer raised a problem, I first had to decide what I wanted the player to experience. We could then discuss how to implement it and build the result into the level. Using the segmented chain became part of the main route. Moving through walls became an optional technique for reaching a collectible. Those two changes helped me decide which actions every player should learn and which to leave for people who want to experiment further.

Looking back, I could have built a small test room earlier to check collisions, pulling and reeling in the chain. That would have helped me catch problems before laying out the entire level and saved rebuilding later.
