Set in a prison devastated by a riot, players switch between two characters, using stealth, environmental puzzles, and a mysterious black box to transfer tools across time. Together, they uncover the prison's secrets and the truth behind their connection.

12 Project Retrospective

What I learned from building a game around a story.

I learned how gameplay and presentation could reshape a story.

When I gave the tower prisoner the power to move guards, I had to ask why they would obey him. The controller in his hand and the shackle at his ankle gave me the captive warden.

Working this way showed me how much of my original plot I could change while keeping the moment I cared about most. I still wanted players to help someone across time and slowly realize they were helping themselves.

Figure text

Guard controller

The guard changes posts.

The ankle shackle

I knew the clues, but I didn’t know how players would connect them.

In the early design, players would observe certain events without understanding their significance, then see their consequences in the next stage. On the following loop, they could intervene at the source—for example, by placing items in a box they would encounter later.

Figure text

ROUND 1 / STATION

ROUND 2 / VALLEY

Leave the wrench?

Empty

When I write a scene, I decide when the character looks up. However, in a game, a player might look straight toward the cabin. Would receiving an empty box in one round give them a reason to leave a knife in it in the next?

What should I do if I want to revisit stage transitions?

I chose free switching because I could test whether players saw their action’s results. However, I didn’t know how to test an inference that took two rounds to form. I’ll need to understand how players interpret each clue, its foreshadowing, and the eventual outcome—whether they can connect them and make deliberate changes in later loops.

“Which detail gave you that idea?”

I want to learn how different players find their way into the same moment. Then I can use the model to revisit the writing, the action, or the space.
