01 BASINHOLD

Set in a prison devastated by a riot, players switch between two characters, using stealth, environmental puzzles, and a mysterious black box to transfer tools across time. Together, they uncover the prison's secrets and the truth behind their connection.

02 Study foucus

I want to turn encounters between different versions of the same person into a playable experience.

Players would discover unexplained connections, help their other self, and gradually understand how their experiences are linked. Their actions would both support the other self and reveal the relationship between them.

03 A Story-Led Holistic Design Process

My five-stage workflow, informed by Steve Lee’s holistic level design approach (GDC 2017).

04 Interative Narrative Approach

I originally designed gameplay based on the story structure I learned from these movies. Players would encounter questions throughout one stage, then gradually uncover answers in later stages.

The core remained: different temporal selves help one another. I redistributed clues about time and space across viewpoints, so players could gradually understand their connection through switching and exploration.

05 Worldbuilding Through Prison Life

I chose a prison as the setting to limit interactions across time and avoid paradoxes. Then I set it in a basin where the cliffs extend the confinement beyond the walls.

From the ground, players can see the bridge leading outside and trace it back to the tower

06 From Solo Play to Cooperation

My story follows one person at two points in time. I gave both selves the same box so that what the earlier one leaves inside is there for the later one to find.

07 Empower the Player to Act with Narrative Intentionality

I designed a handheld controller that shows guard posts. From the tower, the player can decide where to send a guard.

Select a new post on the map. The guard changes position. Back on the ground, the player still has to judge when to cross.

08 Story Is About People

The prisoners fled in the riot. Rain brought down the forest slope, killing the escapees.

His aid was discovered. Pushed off the cliff by the guards, he survived but lost his memory.

09 The Courtyard and the Tower - Level Walkthrough

The guard overlooks the gap between two walls. Pick the next cover first.

10 How the Prison Shapes Player Choice

I use gaps and corners to give players a place to watch the guards turn.

I show players three destinations from inside the tower: the road, the stairs and the power room.

11 Testing the Design at Player Scale

I wanted the cliff walls to enclose the prison and the tower bridge to mark the way out.

Could I spot the way out, judge when to cross, and see what the tower had changed for me on the ground?

12 Project Retrospective

When I gave the tower prisoner the power to move guards, I had to ask why they would obey him. The controller in his hand and the shackle at his ankle gave me the captive warden.

Working this way showed me how much of my original plot I could change while keeping the moment I cared about most. I still wanted players to help someone across time and slowly realize they were helping themselves.
