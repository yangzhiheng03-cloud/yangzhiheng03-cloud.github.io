ALIVE!

2025.07
Overview
Give instructions to everyday objects, then press play. Across seven puzzles, their movements and interactions help an autonomous character reach the exit.
Goals
Help players reason about timing. The early rooms isolate one interaction at a time, then later puzzles ask players to make the result of one action set up the next.
Engine
Unity
Genre
Turn-based programming puzzle
Project duration
2 days
Team & production
Five-person CiGA Game Jam team: two designers and three programmers. I designed gameplay, levels and narrative.

INSPIRATION & RESEARCH

INSPIRATION

The theme Alive reminded me of Roles Reversed, the GMTK 2023 theme. Could objects normally used by the hero come to life and act on their own?

I shifted control from the hero to the room’s objects and separated planning from execution. Players arrange the objects’ actions in advance, then watch their plan unfold as they help the character escape.

THEME DEVELOPMENT

CASE STUDY / BABA IS YOU

01 Opening puzzle · Make the goal, obstacle and tools legible

① FLAG IS WIN sets the goal. ② ROCK IS PUSH makes rocks movable. ③ WALL IS STOP blocks passage. A short route brings all three together.

Design takeaway · Present the goal and available tools together, and keep the first solution direct.

02 Out of Reach · Turn one result into the next prerequisite

① Sink a rock to cross the water. ② Reach the ROCK IS PUSH rule. ③ Replace PUSH with WIN, then touch another rock to finish.

Design takeaway · Let an intermediate result open a new possibility and suggest the next step.

03 Research Facility · Use autonomous movement to unlock indirectly

① Locate the bolt and form BOLT IS MOVE. ② Replace the skulls’ HOT with DEFEAT so the bolt can pass, push text and break the lethal rule. ③ Retrieve WIN and form BOLT IS WIN.

Design takeaway · Make the acting object, its target and their dependencies visible in the puzzle.

PERSONA / EXPECTED PLAYERS

Player 1 / Rule discovery

Enjoys rule-based puzzles such as Baba Is You. Observes how objects relate, proposes solutions and tests them.

Design response

Show goals, available tools and prerequisites.

Player 2 / Planning

Likes planning several steps ahead and watching a chain of results. Uses failure feedback to revise a plan.

Design response

Make turns and action outcomes easy to follow.

DESIGN INTENTION

Start with familiar object functions, then add prerequisites, positioning and timing. Progress toward coordinating events so players can see their plans take effect.

MY CONTRIBUTION & DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

WORK OVERVIEW

In the 2-day 2025 CIGA Game Jam, I handled gameplay, level and narrative design in a five-person team.

Alive, GMTK 2023’s Roles Reversed and Baba Is You led me to a core loop: plan objects’ moves and actions, then watch them execute.

I built puzzles around goals, barriers, tools and solutions. Titles, layouts, route previews and failure feedback communicated them. I designed seven levels and their grid layouts.

With the programmers, I defined a fixed action order. Feedback from teammates and jam players informed my review of the final level and plans for iteration.

01 PLAN THE LEVEL PROGRESSION

Introduce new relationships in stages, from unlocking to prerequisites, combinations and interacting events.

Progression · Prerequisites → Combinations → Parallel events

PLAYER FEEDBACK / THE FINAL LEVEL

I watched teammates and jam players play. Most described the final level as too difficult. The step from individual mechanics to their combined use was too large.

02 BUILD PUZZLES ON A GRID

Place objects, barriers and meeting points on a grid. Count movement and interaction turns, then compare the plan with the playable version.

03 AGREE ON THE ACTION ORDER

I broke each mechanic into checks the code could perform. The programmers suggested implementation changes and provided editing tools for level design.

04 REFLECTION & ITERATION

Distinguish difficulty factors; allow for revision

Parallel events do not add difficulty linearly

I treated each extra parallel event as a simple difficulty increase. Players also had to coordinate its effects and timing with other events. This differs from adding distance or prerequisite steps.

Assess each parameter separately, then tune the combination using player feedback.

NEXT STEPS

REFINE LEVEL COMBINATIONS

The jam delivered playable mechanics and seven levels. Next, vary layouts, prerequisites and event combinations separately to locate learning obstacles.
