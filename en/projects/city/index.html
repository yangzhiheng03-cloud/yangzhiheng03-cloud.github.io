The City’s Hidden Side

  • UE5
  • Open world
  • Stealth
  • Spatial design

The same museum serves as a public attraction by day and an infiltration target by night. Over nine days I designed the city and museum layouts, built the graybox, and implemented door and elevator interactions in Blueprints.

Finding the first destination

A north–south road divides the 500 m square district. From the elevated plaza the nearby museum gives players their first destination. The school beyond is a place to cleanse recovered objects, while the distant church spire helps orient the player. The museum offers a natural visitor route for teaching and service areas for alternate approaches.

Reconnaissance across four levels

The museum building measures about 90 × 60 m. Three floors connect to an underground escape level. A daytime visit reveals exhibits and restricted entrances. At night, a passage between the restrooms approaches Gallery 8 from behind, while the safe room’s lift connects an objective to an escape route.

Choosing an entrance means planning an exit

The front entrance follows the familiar visitor route but requires some upstairs backtracking. The loading area lets players work against the visitor flow. The underground route is the most concealed, although it does not serve every collectible directly. The mission design raises the alarm after collection, giving shortcuts spotted by day a purpose during escape.

What the prototype tests

The playable graybox tests scale, access and basic interactions, including working doors and elevators. Cameras, guard perception and pursuit, the day/night transition and the global alarm are specified in the mission design for further implementation.
