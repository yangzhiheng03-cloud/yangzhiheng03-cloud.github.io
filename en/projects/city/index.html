The City’s Hidden Side

2026.04
Overview
The player is a student in a city affected by cognitive contamination. They scout the museum by day and return at night. The museum has six collection sites. Once the player collects at least four fragments, they can leave the museum. Purifying the fragments back at school completes the mission.
Goals
Players should be able to choose an entrance from what they saw by day and judge when another exit is worth the detour.
Engine
Unreal Engine 5
Genre
Open-world exploration and stealth
Project duration
9 days
Team & production
Solo project. I designed the city and museum layouts, built the graybox and implemented doors and elevators.

Daytime clues, nighttime decisions

A visitor can see staff using a side door without knowing where it leads. I used that gap between visibility and access to give players a reason to investigate during an ordinary visit.

Separate discovery from collection

Objects can be identified by day but collected only at night. That delay gives players a reason to scout entrances and decide which object to collect first. I place the side door and the guarded object within the same view so the player can judge the distance between them.

Keep routes open as risk changes

More guards cover the main exit as the alert rises. Players can risk the shortcut or take a longer route before returning to school to purify the fragments. The public route passes a second exit and reveals which way it leads.

From the hilltop plaza to the museum

I took the central square and the surrounding streets of Kraków’s Old Town as a reference for a 500 × 500 m district. The terrain is mostly flat. A north–south road divides the district, and the hilltop plaza at its southern edge gives the player a first view across the city.

A reason to return to the centre

The student lives in a city where contaminated objects distort the residents’ judgement. The museum’s treasures, the bank’s gold, the church’s Bible and the factory’s engineering documents have all become objects of excessive protection. When the student recovers an object, the people guarding it try to take it back. The student must bring it to the school for purification.

In the district plan, the museum teaches the first mission along a visitor route. The bank introduces a more heavily guarded area beyond its public counters. The church takes the approach up to the roof, and the factory uses long passages for a chase. The park is a starting point for the night visit. A 20 m main road links districts, 12 m roads connect local sites and 4 m pedestrian routes lead to individual entrances.

The public galleries and the staff route

I chose a museum for the first mission because the visitor route gives a newcomer a sequence to follow. Behind the galleries, exhibits also need to be delivered, restored and stored. I studied the organisation of the Saint Louis Art Museum, then laid out my own building around the places where those two uses meet.

The museum occupies a 120 × 120 m site. The 90 × 60 m main building has three above-ground floors and a basement. The foyer, galleries and loading bay share the ground floor. The upper galleries, conservation studio and office occupy the second floor. Collection storage leads to the secure vault on the third. The basement connects the lifts to the parking aisles and the route back to the street.

Daytime reconnaissance

In the mission “Clues by Day, Treasures by Night”, a student visits the City Civilisation Treasures exhibition. The six glowing spheres in the foyer indicate how many fragments remain. In the galleries, staff intervene when a visitor gets too close to certain pieces. At the edge of the conservation area, the student can see a destination but is stopped from entering. By the end of the visit, the player should have identified likely targets and at least two ways out.

Three approaches to the night mission

Front entrance · follow the visitor route

The recommended first run starts along the route the player knows by day. Gallery 1 introduces crouching, the corridor in Gallery 2 introduces moving between cover, and Gallery 3 holds the first object. The mezzanine introduces a stun from behind before Gallery 5 puts the next object among several guards. From Gallery 7 the route continues to the grand gallery. Its return stair leads up to Gallery 8, then on through the conservation studio and storage to the vault.

Loading bay · reach the conservation studio first

Entering by the side door or loading bay brings the player to the conservation studio before much of the public exhibition. It shortens the return journey, but puts the player in a restricted area earlier, with closer patrol and camera coverage in the mission plan. The studio connects to the office and storage as well as to the loading bay.

Basement lift · reverse the order

The basement approach starts at the vault end. The player can work back through collection storage and the studio to the galleries, then leave via the loading bay or side door. A smaller shortcut changes a single encounter: the guard in Gallery 8 faces the approach from Gallery 7. The passage between the first- and second-floor restrooms allows the player to come out behind that display.

Leaving the museum and returning to school

The museum has six collection sites: Gallery 3, Gallery 5, the conservation studio, Gallery 8, the grand gallery and the vault. Each holds one fragment. A sphere in the foyer goes dark with each pickup. Once the player has collected at least four fragments, they can leave the museum.

If the player collects the fragment in the vault, the lights turn red, the alarm sounds and reinforcements arrive from the foyer and service area. Lift E1 beside the vault connects the third floor to the basement car park and the street. Whichever exit the player takes, the mission ends when they purify the collected fragments back at school.

The playable blockout

In nine days I completed the district plan, the museum layouts and the UE5 blockout. I used Blockout Tools to check scale and the connections between entrances and floors. Doors and lifts use Blueprints: a trigger enables input, a Gate controls when it is accepted, FlipFlop switches the state, and a Timeline moves the door or platform.

I used AI to explore possible sites and review the design documents. I selected the rules, layouts and implementation. The day/night transition, camera sweeps, guard perception and pursuit, collection-triggered alarm and mission states remain part of the design. For a first playtest, I would record what the player noticed on the day visit, which entrance they chose at night, and where they stopped or turned back.
